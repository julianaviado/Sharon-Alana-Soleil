WHO I WORK WITH

Helping others comes naturally. Helping yourself is another story.

You’re allowed to come first. You don’t have to hold it all.

Many cultures raise you to put family first, always before yourself. You give and give and give, until you abandon yourself. Filipinos have a word for the pull: utang na loob, a quiet debt of honor to the people who sacrificed for you. And one for the cost: naubos, poured out, empty.

But gratitude was never meant to become a lifetime debt. Somewhere, it turned into guilt, and love started to feel like something you owe.

You can honor where you come from without abandoning yourself. You still belong, even when you say no.

How it works

Loving people shouldn’t cost you yourself.

There’s another way to live, and it doesn’t ask you to do more. The pressure isn’t coming from them. It’s coming from you.

If overthinking, overgiving, overachieving, or overextending have become your default way of moving through life, you are not alone—and you are likely experiencing Over Syndrome™.

Over Syndrome™

noun

The internal pressure to do more, give more, and expect more of yourself until you lose sight of what you need and what matters most.

OverthinkingYou replay every conversation, and can’t turn your brain off.
OvergivingYou give until you’re naubos: poured out, nothing left for you.
OverachievingNothing ever feels like enough, no matter what you accomplish.
OverextendingYou say yes before you’ve asked yourself whether you can.
Over-independentYou’d rather do it all yourself than risk needing anyone.

Meet Sharon

Life has a way of revealing what matters most.

When doctors couldn't explain what was happening, they offered Sharon little hope. For a while, she almost believed them.

At 26, a sudden health crisis left her in a wheelchair, facing the possibility that she might never walk again. Then something deep within her whispered,

"Don't give up. This isn't the end of the story."

That journey changed far more than her health. It revealed something Sharon now believes with all her heart:

"The experiences we wish never happened can become the very ones that awaken us to what matters most... and bring us back to the person pressure made us forget."

"Those discoveries live in the Freedom to Flow Method™, the approach Sharon now shares through her speaking and coaching to help people remember who they are beneath the pressure so they can live and enjoy what matters most."
