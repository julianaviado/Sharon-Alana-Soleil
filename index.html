More often than we realize, we get to choose when that time begins.

Pressure Doesn’t Always Feel Like Pressure

Life gives us plenty of pressure in our everyday lives. But not all of that pressure comes from what’s happening around us.

You’re responsible, hardworking, helpful, caring, and dependable.

These strengths get reinforced because people value them. And over time, we start expecting ourselves to always be the person everyone knows they can count on.

Being responsible can turn into feeling responsible for too much.

Being dependable can start to mean never letting anyone down.

I call this Over Syndrome™.

Over Syndrome™

noun

a pattern of thoughts, feelings, and behaviors characterized by the internal pressure to do more, give more, and expect more of yourself, often at your own expense.

Which Pressure Pattern shows up most for you?

  1. Overgiving Giving so much that there’s little left for you.
  2. Overachieving Accomplishing a lot, but never feeling like it’s enough.
  3. Overthinking Thinking things through long after it’s helpful.
  4. Overextending Taking on more than you have room for.
  5. Overindependent Handling things alone when you don’t have to.

You may see yourself in more than one. But under pressure, one usually takes the lead.

Take the quiz to understand your pattern and learn a simple way to put less pressure on yourself.

  • Time with those you love
  • Heart-to-hearts you keep meaning to have
  • Laughing until your stomach hurts
  • Trips you’ve always wanted to take

When you’re used to being there for everyone else, what matters to you can keep getting pushed to later.

That’s the real cost of pressure: it’s missing opportunities to be present for the people, passions, and experiences you don’t want to look back on and wish you had made more time for.

These are the moments you don’t want to miss. And the more present you are for them now, the fewer “I wish I hads” and the more “I’m so glad I dids” you’ll have.

You don’t have to wait for someday to make time for what matters most.

Life may still bring pressure. But pressure doesn’t have to decide how you live.

Meet Sharon

At 26, a sudden health crisis left Sharon in a wheelchair, facing the possibility that she might never walk again.

That experience changed far more than her health. It changed what she valued and showed her the power of being present.

What she once wished had never happened became one of her greatest teachers and helped develop the foundation of her Freedom to Flow Method™. Today, Sharon shares that approach through speaking and coaching, helping people to have more time, energy, and presence for what matters most.
